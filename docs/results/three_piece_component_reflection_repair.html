Every Three-Piece Obstruction Has a Component-Reflection Repair

Name: Every Three-Piece Obstruction Has a Component-Reflection Repair

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Invariants:

  • Shattered Complex

Operations:

  • Graph Expansion
  • Peripheral Expansion

Result Type: Construction

Statement:

Let $X=A_1\cup_DA_2\cup_DA_3$ be an ample forbidden pc-minor in its canonical three-piece presentation, let $e$ be the direction of the common edge $D$, and put $r=\pi_eD$. Some piece $A_i$ has both $U=\pi_eA_i$ and $C=A_i^e$ peelable to $r$. For either $b\in\{0,1\}$, replacing $A_i$ by the peripheral expansion $P_b=(U\times\{b\})\cup(C\times\{1-b\})$ produces a dismantlable ample class $X_b$ with the same shattered sets and cardinality as $X$. The replacement reflects only components of $(\pi_eX)\setminus X^e$ lying in the selected piece. If $k_0$ and $k_1$ are the numbers of its components currently labelled zero and one, one of the two repairs uses $\min(k_0,k_1)$ reflections. This is an upper bound; intermediate reflections need not preserve forbiddenness and the bound need not be optimal.

Status: Established

Proof:

Three-piece boundary necessity supplies a piece admitting an endpoint sink and an endpoint leaf exit, possibly in different acyclic USOs. Contracting the common direction in the sink-ending order makes $U$ peel to $r$. Recording the first lost endpoint of each full fibre in the leaf-exit order gives a corner peeling of $C$ to $r$. In $P_b$, first peel the copy of $C$ in layer $1-b$ to $r$, using the untouched copy of $U$ for mate cubes, and then peel $U\setminus\{r\}$ in layer $b$; this peels the selected piece to $D$. In the full union it leaves an unchanged positive two-piece proper convex minor, so $X_b$ peels. The projected pieces meet only at $r\in X^e$ and have no cross edges, so every complementary component lies in one piece. The component-expansion theorem therefore preserves ampleness, shadow, and cardinality during the relabelling. The two constant labels change respectively $k_1$ and $k_0$ components.
