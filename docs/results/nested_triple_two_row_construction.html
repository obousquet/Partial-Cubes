Nested Ample Triples Produce Two Nonrelative Enlarged Rows

Name: Nested Ample Triples Produce Two Nonrelative Enlarged Rows

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Nonconstant-Row Enlargement
  • Peripheral Expansion
  • Full Reduction
  • Partial-Cube Minor

Result Type: Construction

Statement:

Let full-support nonempty ample $H=(B_0,B_1)$ have contraction $C$, and choose ample $C\subseteq D\subseteq E$. Two successive one-row enlargements admit a marked two-row presentation $Z$ with rows $U_0=(B_0,C)$, $R_0=(D,D)$, $R_1=(E,C)$, and $U_1=(D,B_1)$. It is ample, has common row intersection $J=(D,C)$, and is forbidden exactly when $H$ is forbidden and $D,E$ are dismantlable ample. Its reduction is a copy of $V(H,D)$, its order is $|H|+2|C|+3|D|+|E|$, and $R_0\searrow J$ exactly when $D\searrow C$, while $R_1\searrow J$ exactly when $E\searrow D$. Hence two successive relative failures yield a forbidden two-row assembly with neither enlarged row peeling to the intersection.

Status: Established

Proof:

First form $X=V(H,D)$. Its contraction has sections $(C,D)$. Enlarge the corresponding row of $X$ to the peripheral family $(E,D)$. Reading the resulting eight rows in the other coordinate order gives exactly $Z$, proving ampleness and the marked identities. Applying the one-row obstruction criterion twice, followed by the peripheral positivity criterion, yields the exact forbiddenness equivalence. Row counting gives the order. Relative deletion in the appropriate product layer lifts $D\searrow C$ and $E\searrow D$; reduction or restriction of any reverse chain recovers the original relative order.
