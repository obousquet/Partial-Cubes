A Two-Unit Wing Excludes a Pair-Poor Extra-Owner Wing

Name: A Two-Unit Wing Excludes a Pair-Poor Extra-Owner Wing

Classes:

  • Ample Classes

Result Type: Implication

Statement:

Let an ample two-layer family have overlap $B$ of order at most nine. If one terminal wing has two-cycle holonomy of excess two and the opposite wing has the one-unit extra-owner form, then the extra-owner wing is pair-rich. Consequently the two wings either supply three pairwise disjoint acquired direction pairs or their four acquired pairs form an alternating $C_4$, a four-edge path, or two disjoint two-edge paths.

Status: Established

Proof:

If the extra-owner wing were pair-poor, $|B|=9$ and $G(B)$ would be one of the two rooted-claw trees, with acquired old-arm support $Q$. Comparing its rigid cuts with all seven two-unit profiles shows that every feasible profile also acquires $Q$: the two-apex and disjoint $2$--$3$ profiles retain the old arm; the intersecting profiles are impossible; and the three vertex-free component profiles either isolate the old arm, contradict the required internal shore edge, or do not occur in either rooted-claw tree. Thus both wings acquire $Q$ although $B$ does not shatter it, contradicting $\operatorname{Sh}(A_0)\cap\operatorname{Sh}(A_1)=\operatorname{Sh}(B)$. Both wings are therefore pair-rich, and the opposite-wing support theorem gives the stated alternatives.
