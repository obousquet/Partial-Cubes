Rooted Factors Are Periphery-Free, Product-Prime, and Stable under Small Corner Deletions

Name: Rooted Factors Are Periphery-Free, Product-Prime, and Stable under Small Corner Deletions

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Peripheral Expansion
  • Cartesian Product
  • Concept Deletion
  • Partial-Cube Minor

Result Type: Implication

Statement:

Every rooted factor is periphery-free and Cartesian-prime. Deleting a nonroot corner of degree at most two preserves rooted-factor status. If two nonroot corners are adjacent and have degree three, deleting either one or both also preserves rooted-factor status. Consequently, if a rooted factor has a nonroot corner but no preserving nonroot-corner deletion, every nonroot corner has degree at least three, and a degree-three nonroot corner has no nonroot corner as a neighbor.

Status: Established

Proof:

A peripheral coordinate would express the family as a peripheral expansion whose contraction base and relevant section are proper rooted minors; their endpoint orders combine to reach the forbidden root. A nontrivial Cartesian product similarly combines root-ending orders of its two factor restrictions. For deletion, glue the factor to a fixed rooted factor to obtain a cut-vertex forbidden pc-minor. Low-degree corner descent, or adjacent degree-three-pair descent, preserves the obstruction; the resulting family still has the same cut vertex, so the cut-vertex classification forces the changed block to remain a rooted factor. Adjacent corners share their unique maximal cube and therefore have equal degree.
