The Pure Two-Unit Holonomy Anomaly Has Two Square Apices

Name: The Pure Two-Unit Holonomy Anomaly Has Two Square Apices

Classes:

  • Ample Classes

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

In the small lower-family two-arm setting, suppose $|K|\le9$, a directed two-cycle has excess $\varepsilon=2$, the uncounted remainder is $\eta=2$, there are exactly two passive directions, every carrier-excess term vanishes, and both cycle shores have order two. Then the active slice has order one, $|K|=9$, and the two uncounted vertices $u,v$ satisfy $G(K)-\{u,v\}=P_7$, the path formed by attaching two pendant three-edge arms to the active vertex. All squares of $K$ are uniquely supported and meet an apex, with $q(K)=\deg_K(u)+\deg_K(v)-\mathbf1_{uv\in E(K)}-2$. At most one of the two terminal arm-support pairs is shattered by $K$, so at least one is acquired by the upper family.

Status: Established

Proof:

Equality in every counted budget term forces disjoint active-edge shores, one passive direction and collar per shore, two pendant three-edge arms, and $|K|=|L|+8$. The shore-root lemma and $|K|\le9$ give $|L|=1$ and $|K|=9$, leaving exactly two apices outside the resulting $P_7$; the two-apex square-complex lemma gives the square structure and count. Any square on a terminal support must contain both apices, because deleting a single apex would leave a forbidden two-edge path in $P_7$. The two terminal directions are distinct, or convexity of their common semicube would identify the disjoint active shores. Thus the terminal supports are disjoint. Two such squares cannot both use the same apex pair, whether the apices are adjacent or opposite, so at most one terminal pair is shattered below; both are shattered above.
