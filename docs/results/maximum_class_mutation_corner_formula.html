Corner Formula for a Maximum-Class Mutation

Name: Corner Formula for a Maximum-Class Mutation

Classes:

  • Maximum Classes
  • Cornerless Ample Classes

Result Type: Invariant Law

Statement:

Let maximum classes $C,C'\subseteq Q_n$ have VC dimension $d$ and differ by the legal exchange $C'=(C\setminus\{r\})\cup\{a\}$. Put $U=(C\setminus\{r\})\cap(N(r)\setminus N(a))$ and $V=(C\setminus\{r\})\cap(N(a)\setminus N(r))$. The incident coordinate sets at $r$ in $C$ and at $a$ in $C'$ agree; writing their common degree as $q$, one has $|\operatorname{Cor}(C')|-|\operatorname{Cor}(C)|=|\{v\in U:\deg_C(v)=d+1\}|-|\{v\in V:\deg_C(v)=d\}|$. Moreover $C'$ is cornerless exactly when $q>d$, every corner of $C$ lies in $V$, and no vertex of $U$ has degree $d+1$. In particular, if $C$ has exactly two corners, a one-mutation move to a cornerless maximum class requires those corners to have Hamming distance two. These conditions evaluate a legal mutation; they do not by themselves prove that the exchanged family is maximum.

Status: Established

Proof:

Every maximal cube of a maximum VC-$d$ class has dimension $d$, each $d$-support carries exactly one cube, and a vertex is a corner exactly when its degree is $d$. Under the exchange, each $d$-cube through $r$ is replaced by the corresponding cube through $a$ and every other cube survives, so the exchanged vertices have the same incident coordinate set. Among surviving vertices, precisely those in $U$ lose one neighbour and precisely those in $V$ gain one. Thus the new surviving corners are the old degree-$(d+1)$ vertices of $U$, while the lost corners are the old degree-$d$ vertices of $V$; $r$ and $a$ contribute equally. This gives the formula and the cornerless criterion. If two old corners are both lost, they are distinct neighbours of $a$ and therefore differ in exactly two coordinates.
