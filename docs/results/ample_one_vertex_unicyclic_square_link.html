A One-Vertex Completion of a Unicyclic Graph Has a Controlled Square Link

Name: A One-Vertex Completion of a Unicyclic Graph Has a Controlled Square Link

Classes:

  • Ample Classes

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

Let $B$ be ample, let $v\in B$, and suppose $T=G(B)-v$ is connected and unicyclic with at most one square. Write $q_0\in\{0,1\}$ for the number of squares in $T$, $d=\deg_B(v)$, and $\Lambda_v$ for the square link at $v$. Then $B$ has no cube of dimension at least three, every square support occurs once, and $B$ has exactly $d$ squares, of which $d-q_0$ contain $v$. If $q_0=0$, $\Lambda_v$ is connected and unicyclic; if $q_0=1$, the unique cycle of $T$ is that square and $\Lambda_v$ is a tree.

Status: Established

Proof:

A three-cube avoiding $v$ leaves six squares in $T$, while one containing $v$ leaves three opposite square faces there, contradicting the hypothesis; connected support carriers then make square supports unique. If $r$ is the number of active directions, $q$ the number of squares, and $n=|B|$, ampleness gives $n=1+r+q$ and $|E(G(B))|=r+2q$. Since the unicyclic graph $T$ has $n-1$ edges and restoring $v$ adds $d$, one gets $q=d$ and $|E(\Lambda_v)|=d-q_0$. A cycle in the link subdivides to a cycle in $T$, so its cycle rank is at most one. The two possible edge counts then force respectively a connected unicyclic link or a tree.
