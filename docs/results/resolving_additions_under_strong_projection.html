Resolving Additions under Strong Projection

Name: Resolving Additions under Strong Projection

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Full Reduction
  • Concept Deletion

Result Type: Invariant Law

Statement:

Let nonempty ample $S\subseteq Q_F$ have an ample one-concept extension $H=S\cup\{a\}$ with acquired support $I$. For $D\subseteq F$, write $a_D=a|_{F\setminus D}$. Then $H^D=S^D\cup\{a_D\}$ when $D\subseteq I$, while $H^D=S^D$ when $D\not\subseteq I$. In the first case $a_D$ is new and has acquired support $I\setminus D$; if $S^D$ is empty, the extension is a singleton and its support is empty. Consequently, if $H$ is dismantlable ample but $S^D$ is not, then $D\subseteq I$ and $|I\setminus D|\ge 3$. In particular, when $|I|=3$, every reduction of $S$ on a nonempty coordinate set is dismantlable ample.

Status: Established

Proof:

Only the fibre through $a$ can become a new full $D$-fibre. If $D\subseteq I$, the punctured $I$-cube supplies all its other concepts, so its projection $a_D$ is newly present. If $D$ contains a coordinate outside $I$, the corresponding neighbour of $a$ is absent and the fibre remains incomplete. In the first case the surviving incident directions are exactly $I\setminus D$, giving the asserted acquired support. Strong projections preserve dismantlable ampleness by ordering full fibres according to their completion times in an ample-prefix order. Hence $H^D$ and $S^D$ must differ whenever the former peels and the latter does not, forcing $D\subseteq I$. Their difference is then a resolving one-concept addition, and rank-two extension invariance forces its support to have size at least three. A nonempty $D$ cannot satisfy this when $|I|=3$.
