Latent Punctured Faces Characterize Corner-Peeling Words

Name: Latent Punctured Faces Characterize Corner-Peeling Words

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

For $H\subseteq Q_F$, let its latent punctured-face system be all pairs $(y,S)$ with $|S|\ge2$ and $\mathsf P(y,S)\subseteq H$; the tip $y$ may be present, representing a punctured face activated when $y$ is deleted. For an ordering $\mathbf x$ of an ample $H$, assign an absent tip position zero and let $b_{\mathbf x}(y,S)$ be the first displayed member of $\mathsf P(y,S)$. Then $\mathbf x$ is a corner peeling exactly when, for every latent block, whenever the tip occurs before its first displayed vertex, that first vertex is a one-coordinate neighbour $y^{\{q\}}$ of the tip for some $q\in S$.

Status: Established

Proof:

In a corner peeling, let the first surviving vertex of a latent block be $x=y^T$ after the tip has disappeared. Immediately before deleting $x$, every other displayed block vertex is present. If $|T|\ge2$, the entire punctured subcube $\mathsf P(y,T)$ remains around $x$ while its tip is absent, so the local cube-support complex at $x$ is not a simplex and $x$ is not a corner. Thus $|T|=1$. Conversely, proceed inductively through an ordering satisfying the word rule. Earlier corner deletions preserve ampleness. If the current vertex were not a corner, a minimal nonface $T$ of its local cube-support complex would produce a latent block with missing or earlier tip $y=x^T$, while the current vertex $y^T$ is the block's first displayed member and $|T|\ge2$, contradicting the rule.
