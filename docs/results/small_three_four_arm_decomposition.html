A Small Three--Four Arm Decomposition Is Intrinsic Except on a Path

Name: A Small Three--Four Arm Decomposition Is Intrinsic Except on a Path

Classes:

  • Partial Cubes

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

Let a connected graph $G$ of order at most nine be obtained from a path core of order one or two by attaching a pendant path of length three (the ordinary arm) and a connected four-vertex tree or four-cycle by one edge at a distinguished vertex. If $G$ has two such decompositions, their ordinary arms coincide except for $P_8$ with a one-vertex core or $P_9$ with a two-vertex core, where the decompositions interchange the length-three and length-four sides. If $G$ also has a sharp decomposition into a path core of order at most three and two pendant length-three arms, every three--four decomposition uses one of those sharp arms as its ordinary arm, with the same attachment and terminal edges.

Status: Established

Proof:

A four-cycle component is the unique nontrivial block and is recovered from the block--cut tree. For a four-vertex tree, the internally rooted path, centre-rooted claw, and leaf-rooted claw have distinct degree and branch-order profiles, which recover the rooted component and leave the ordinary arm. The only ambiguous rooted component is a path rooted at an endpoint. Attaching paths of lengths three and four to a one-vertex core gives $P_8$; attaching them to opposite ends of a two-vertex core gives $P_9$; attaching them to the same end creates a unique degree-three vertex with branch lengths $1,3,4$. Comparison with the intrinsic sharp-arm decomposition then identifies the ordinary arm in all remaining cases.
