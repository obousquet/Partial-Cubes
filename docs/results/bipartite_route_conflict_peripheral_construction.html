Bipartite Route Conflicts Give a Convex-Treelike Construction

Name: Bipartite Route Conflicts Give a Convex-Treelike Construction

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Convex-Treelike Partial Cubes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Invariants:

  • Missing-Pattern Conflict Graph
  • Attainable Sink Set

Operations:

  • Peripheral Expansion

Result Type: Construction

Statement:

If a nonempty ample family has a bipartite full missing-pattern conflict graph, then it is obtained recursively from singletons by peripheral expansions $(B\times\{0\})\cup(S\times\{1\})$ with $\varnothing\ne S\subseteq B$ and both children constructed by the same rule; constant coordinates may be added freely. Thus it is convex-treelike and every vertex is an attainable acyclic-USO sink. The recursion is recovered from the signed patterns by repeatedly choosing a coordinate at which at most one literal occurs. Consequently every ample forbidden pc-minor for either the convex-treelike or dismantlable ample class, and every rooted obstruction factor, has a nonbipartite conflict graph.

Status: Established

Proof:

In a bipartition, if both literal values occurred at every active coordinate, all routes in one part would restrict one common word and all routes in the other its opposite. Signed elimination of a conflicting pair would then produce a route properly contained in one parent, contradicting that routes form a clutter. Hence some coordinate uses at most one polarity, which makes one section contain the other. Conflict-graph monotonicity preserves bipartiteness in both sections, so induction gives the peripheral construction and every endpoint sink. Contraposition gives the obstruction statement.

Comments:

Bipartiteness is sufficient, not necessary: path graphs can have nonbipartite missing-pattern conflict graphs while remaining convex-treelike.
