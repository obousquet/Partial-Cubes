The Rooted Signed Graph Presents the Relative Yang Module

Name: The Rooted Signed Graph Presents the Relative Yang Module

Classes:

  • Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

Let $A\subsetneq B\subseteq Q_F$ be ample, $D=B\setminus A$, $S_F=k[v_{q,0},v_{q,1}:q\in F]$, $m_x=\prod_{q\in F}v_{q,1-x_q}$, $J_X=(m_x:x\in X)$, and $\mathcal Q(B,A)=J_B/J_A$. The map $\varphi:\bigoplus_{x\in D}S_F\varepsilon_x\to\mathcal Q(B,A)$ sending $\varepsilon_x$ to $\overline{m_x}$ has kernel generated by the root relations $v_{q,x_q}\varepsilon_x$ for $x^{\{q\}}\in A$ and the internal-edge relations $v_{q,x_q}\varepsilon_x-v_{q,y_q}\varepsilon_y$ for $y=x^{\{q\}}\in D$. Hence the coordinate- and sign-labelled rooted graph $\widehat G(B,A)$ determines the relative module with its distinguished generators and all relative shelling orders. More explicitly, put $L_A(x)=\{q:x^{\{q\}}\in A\}$. Delete every internal or root edge whose coordinate label belongs to $L_A(x)$. Then $A\cup\{x\}$ is ample exactly when $x$ and the root lie in different components of the remaining graph.

Status: Established

Proof:

The relative Yang face module is Cohen--Macaulay, so its squarefree Alexander dual $\mathcal Q(B,A)$ has a linear resolution and is linearly presented. In the general monomial presentation, the linear annihilator relations are exactly cube edges from $D$ into $A$, and the linear pair relations are exactly cube edges within $D$; these generate the full kernel and are read directly from the signed rooted graph. Linear quotients of the distinguished generators are Alexander dual to relative shellings. For the cut test, the colon ideal $J_A:m_x$ is generated by the products over $\operatorname{Diff}(x,a)$ for $a\in A$, with linear generators precisely $v_{q,x_q}$ for $q\in L_A(x)$. A surviving path from $x$ to the root gives an $a$ agreeing with $x$ on all of $L_A(x)$ and obstructs variable generation. Conversely, any such obstructing $a$ gives, in its signed multidegree, an expression of the unit vector at $x$ by edge differences and rooted unit vectors, forcing the component of $x$ to contain a surviving root edge.
