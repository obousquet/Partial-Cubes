Paired One-Hole Criterion for Cornerless Two-Layer Gluings

Name: Paired One-Hole Criterion for Cornerless Two-Layer Gluings

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Cornerless Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Coordinate Conditioning

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

Let $B_0,B_1\subseteq Q_F$ and $H=(B_0\times\{0\})\cup(B_1\times\{1\})$, and suppose the nonempty family $H$ satisfies Sandwich equality. Define $\operatorname{Int}(B_b)$ as the concepts lying in the interior of an absent-tip punctured affine cube contained in $B_b$. Define $\operatorname{Cross}_b(B_0,B_1)$ as the common concepts $a\in B_0\cap B_1$ for which some nonempty support $T$ gives a full $T$-cube through $a$ in $B_b$ and the same cube punctured at $a^T$ in $B_{1-b}$. Then $H$ is cornerless exactly when $B_b=\operatorname{Int}(B_b)\cup\operatorname{Cross}_b(B_0,B_1)$ for both $b=0,1$. All protection sets are computed directly from full and punctured affine cubes in the sections, giving an ordering-free construction and recovery criterion for the nonpeelable root of a two-layer gluing.

Status: Established

Proof:

Sandwich equality makes $H$ ample. By the local-hole characterization, a lifted concept $(x,b)$ is not a corner exactly when it lies in the interior of a punctured cube with a missing tip. If the punctured-cube support avoids the layer coordinate, the whole block lies in layer $b$ and gives precisely internal protection of $x$. If the support contains the layer coordinate, deleting that coordinate leaves a nonempty support $T$: the layer-$b$ section is the full $T$-cube through $x$, while the opposite section is the same cube punctured at $x^T$. This is exactly cross-layer protection, and either construction reverses to the corresponding punctured cube in $H$. Therefore every concept in both layers is a noncorner exactly when the two displayed protection equalities hold.

This is the exact nonpeelability compatibility law for the two sections. It does not assert that the elementary pc-minors peel; intrinsic attachment conditions ensuring those positive images remain a separate problem.
