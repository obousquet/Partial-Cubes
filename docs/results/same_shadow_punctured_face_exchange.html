Same-Shadow Exchange at a Punctured Face

Name: Same-Shadow Exchange at a Punctured Face

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion

Result Type: Invariant Law

Statement:

Let $S$ be a cornerless ample forbidden pc-minor for the dismantlable ample classes, let $(a,I)$ be punctured-face data for $S$, and put $A=S\cup\{a\}$. Define $\mathcal E_S(a,I)$ to consist of the nontrivial $I$-flips $a^U$ that have no neighbour in $S$ in a direction outside $I$. Then the corners of $A$ are exactly the disjoint union of $\{a\}$ and $\mathcal E_S(a,I)$. For every exposed tip $c$, the exchange $T_c=(S\setminus\{c\})\cup\{a\}$ is ample and has the same shattered-set complex as $S$. The datum $(a,I)$ is peelable exactly when $T_c$ is dismantlable ample for some exposed tip $c$. No exposed tip is at Hamming distance one from $a$. If $|I|=2$, every $T_c$ is isomorphic to $S$, so the datum is obstruction-preserving; consequently every peelable punctured-face datum over a cornerless forbidden family has $|I|\ge 3$.

Status: Established

Proof:

The new tip $a$ is a corner of $A$ with completed $I$-cube as its unique maximal cube. Any other corner must have a maximal cube containing $a$, since otherwise it would already be a corner of the cornerless family $S$; this identifies it exactly with an exposed tip. Deleting such a corner removes the unique newly shattered support $I$, so the exchanged family is ample with the same shadow as $S$. A peeling of $A$ cannot start by deleting $a$, because that leaves nonpeelable $S$; its other possible first vertices are precisely the exposed tips, proving the exchange criterion. A distance-one exposed tip would already be a corner of $S$. For $|I|=2$, the only possible exposed tip is opposite $a$ in the completed square, and swapping the two opposite vertices is an automorphism of $A$, so every exchange is isomorphic to $S$ and cannot peel.
