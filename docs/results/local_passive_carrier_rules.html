Local Carrier Rules Through Residual Degree Three

Name: Local Carrier Rules Through Residual Degree Three

Classes:

  • Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion

Result Type: Implication

Statement:

Let $K\subsetneq A$ be ample, put $R=A\setminus K$ and $\mathcal H=\operatorname{Sh}(A)\setminus\operatorname{Sh}(K)$, and let $c\in R$ be doubly blocked: neither $K\cup\{c\}$ nor $A\setminus\{c\}$ is ample. Compatible lower carriers of minimal supports and upper carriers of maximal supports meet in a full residual cube. The concept $c$ lies in at least two carriers on each shore. At residual degree two, $c$ marks an isolated support containing exactly one incident residual direction, and adjacent markers reverse ownership of their common direction. At residual degree three, $c$ marks either an isolated support or a minimal-maximal support cover whose difference has size one.

Status: Established

Proof:

Complement duality turns every relative support into a product of lower and upper trace-defect classes inside $R$; singleton defect classes characterize minimal and maximal supports, yielding the cross-carrier cube. Double blocking forces at least two minimal obstruction signatures on each shore. After translating $c$ to zero, existential-universal reductions produce two nonempty disjoint ample classes on the residual directions. For two directions these are disjoint intervals in a three-vertex path, forcing an isolated support and adjacent-marker reversal. For three directions, the exhaustive local two-shore calculation supplies disjoint minimal transversals; their union leaves at most one coordinate, giving the isolated-or-cover dichotomy.
