Seed-Corner Deletions in Signed-Facet Assemblies

Name: Seed-Corner Deletions in Signed-Facet Assemblies

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion
  • Full Reduction
  • Partial-Cube Minor

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

Let $S$ be an ample forbidden pc-minor, let $\mathcal A$ be compatible resolving tips with distinct acquired supports, and let a surjection $\lambda:E\times\{0,1\}\to\mathcal A$ assign opposite facets of every nonempty layer coordinate set $E$ to distinct owners. Put $R_a=\bigcup_{\lambda(e,i)=a}\{t\in Q_E:t_e=i\}$ and $X=(S\times Q_E)\cup\bigcup_{a\in\mathcal A}(\{a\}\times R_a)$. Then $X$ is an ample forbidden pc-minor. For a seed corner $s$, put $G_s=S\setminus\{s\}$ and let $\mathcal A_s$ consist of tips $a$ that are nonadjacent to $s$ and satisfy $G_s\cup\{a\}\in\mathsf{Damp}$. Every obstruction-preserving corner deletion of $X$ deletes a seed concept $(s,t)$, and it does so exactly when $G_s$ is a forbidden pc-minor and $\lambda(e,t_e)\in\mathcal A_s$ for every $e\in E$. For fixed $s$, the allowed layer words form the face $D_s=\prod_{e\in E}\{i:\lambda(e,i)\in\mathcal A_s\}$. Hence $X$ is corner-descent terminal exactly when, for every corner $s$ with forbidden $G_s$, some coordinate $e$ has neither owner in $\mathcal A_s$. In particular, a corner-descent-terminal seed produces terminal signed-facet assemblies even when the seed has corners.

Status: Established

Proof:

The common-seed theorem gives ampleness and forbiddenness. A seed occurrence $(s,t)$ is a corner precisely when $s$ is a seed corner and no repair tip adjacent to $s$ occurs at $t$. Occurrence-row peelings avoid such a deleted concept and remain valid under every elementary pc-minor. If deleting $(s,t)$ preserves forbiddenness, strong projection gives forbidden $G_s$, and restricting to each selected facet then projecting the remaining layers gives the peelable repair $G_s\cup\{\lambda(e,t_e)\}$; this proves necessity. Conversely, the stated repair conditions make $(s,t)$ a corner, leave the deletion nonpeelable through its reduction $G_s$, and supply explicit row and product peelings for every old-coordinate minor, layer contraction, and layer restriction. Thus all proper elementary minors peel. An occurrence-row corner belongs to one uniquely owned facet, so its deletion cannot preserve forbiddenness. The independent coordinate choices give the product face $D_s$, whose emptiness is exactly the opposite-owner terminality test.

Comments:

This is a complete terminality test within a given signed-facet assembly once the seed-deletion and inherited-repair data are known. It is not an intrinsic characterization of which seeds and repair sets are admissible. The fixed certificate checks the corner formula on 602- and 1,202-concept Hall assemblies and gives peelings for all 78 elementary images of two positive deletions.
