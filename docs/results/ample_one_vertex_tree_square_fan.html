An Ample One-Vertex Completion of a Tree Is a Square Fan

Name: An Ample One-Vertex Completion of a Tree Is a Square Fan

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Trees

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

Let $B$ be ample, let $v\in B$, and suppose $T=G(B)-v$ is a nonempty tree. If $d=\deg_{G(B)}(v)$ and the square link $\Lambda_v$ has the $d$ directions incident with $v$ as vertices, adjacent exactly when their square through $v$ belongs to $B$, then $\Lambda_v$ is a tree. Every cube of dimension at least two is a square through $v$, $B$ has exactly $d-1$ squares, every direction not incident with $v$ labels one edge, and an incident direction $i$ labels $1+\deg_{\Lambda_v}(i)$ edges.

Status: Established

Proof:

A square avoiding $v$ would be a cycle in $T$, and a cube of dimension at least three has a square face avoiding $v$. Connectedness of each coordinate carrier in an ample family gives the asserted edge multiplicities and makes the square link a forest: a link cycle would subdivide to a cycle in $T$. If $r$ is the number of active directions and $q=|E(\Lambda_v)|$, carrier counting gives $|E(G(B))|=r+2q$, while the tree completion gives $|E(G(B))|=|B|-2+d$. Ampleness and the absence of larger cubes give $|B|=1+r+q$. Hence $q=d-1$, so the forest on $d$ vertices is a tree.
