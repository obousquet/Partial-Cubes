Coordinate Blocks Produce Arbitrarily Many Maximal Descent Supports

Name: Coordinate Blocks Produce Arbitrarily Many Maximal Descent Supports

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Coordinate-Block Replacement
  • Full Reduction

Result Type: Invariant Law

Statement:

Let $W_E(H)$ replace a coordinate $z$ of $H$ by a nonempty coordinate block $E$, and let $D$ be a set of old coordinates and $T\subseteq E$. If $T$ is proper in $E$, then the reduction on $D\cup T$ is $W_{E\setminus T}(H^D)$; if $T=E$, it is $H^{D\cup\{z\}}$. Hence the reduction is nonpeelable exactly when the indicated reduction of $H$ is nonpeelable. In particular, if $H$ is an ample forbidden pc-minor whose every nonempty-coordinate reduction peels, then the nonpeelable reductions of $W_E(H)$ are exactly the proper subsets of $E$. For $|E|=m$, there are exactly $m$ maximal descent supports $E\setminus\{e\}$, every maximal one-coordinate negative descent has length $m-1$, and every endpoint is $H$ with its marked coordinate renamed.

Status: Established

Proof:

Reduction in old coordinates commutes with block replacement. The only nontrivial section identity is $(B_0\cup B_1)^D=B_0^D\cup B_1^D$: any full $D$-cube in the union is shattered by one of the two sections and therefore lies wholly in it. Reducing a proper subset of block coordinates leaves the same constant and nonconstant fibre pattern on the remaining block; reducing all of them requires the old word to lie in the original reduction. The block peeling equivalence transfers nonpeelability. Under the terminal-reduction hypothesis on $H$, only $D=\varnothing$ can remain negative in the proper-block case, while the full-block case is positive. The maximal proper subsets of an $m$-element block give the stated count, lengths, and endpoints.

Comments:

This disproves uniqueness of maximal descent support and any uniform bound on descent length for general forbidden three-cores with two-connected crossing graphs. It does not disprove uniqueness of the terminal isomorphism type.
