Corner Deletion Followed by Rooted Minor Descent

Name: Corner Deletion Followed by Rooted Minor Descent

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion
  • Partial-Cube Minor

Result Type: Algorithm

Statement:

Let $(H,a)$ be a rooted factor and let $x\ne a$ be a corner such that $S=H\setminus\{x\}$ is corner-peelable. Some root-preserving pc-minor $(C,b)=(\mu S,\mu a)$ is a rooted factor with $|C|<|H|$. If $(S,a)$ is not already rooted-minimal, $\mu$ is proper and $\mu H=C\cup\{d\}$ is an ample one-concept repair peeling to $b$. If $P$ is the support of the corner cube at $x$ and a normal form for $\mu$ restricts $R$ and contracts $Q$, then $Q\cap P=\varnothing$, every restriction in $R$ keeps the root bit, the acquired support at $d$ is $P\setminus R$, and $|P\setminus R|\ge3$. Iteration ends at a rooted factor whose root is the unique source in every acyclic USO.

Status: Established

Proof:

The deletion remains ample, fails to peel to the root, and is ordinarily peelable. Minor-minimize that rooted failure. If the chosen map is proper, its images of $H$ and $S$ differ in exactly the repair $d$. A restriction discarding $x$, or a contraction in an incident direction, would identify the images, proving the normal-form exclusions. Shattered-support transport identifies the repair support with $P\setminus R$; support at most two cannot change endpoint peelability. Cardinality decreases at every iteration. At termination no peeling can start away from the root, which is equivalent through the peeling–acyclic-USO correspondence to unique-source status in every acyclic USO.
