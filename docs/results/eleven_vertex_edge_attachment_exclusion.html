Edge Attachments Cannot Create Endpoint-Retention Failure on the Eleven-Vertex Core

Name: Edge Attachments Cannot Create Endpoint-Retention Failure on the Eleven-Vertex Core

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Common-Section Gluing
  • Full Reduction

Result Type: Implication

Statement:

In four coordinates of weights $1,2,4,8$, let $S=\{0,1,2,3,7,8,9,11,12,13,15\}$ and $T=\{0,8\}$. Attach arbitrary positive ample pieces to distinct edges of $S$ whose direction is not $8$, using disjoint private coordinates. If the assembled family peels to both $0$ and $8$, then it peels to the edge $T$. Its reduction in direction $8$ remains the path $0-1-3-7$. Hence this entire edge-attachment template cannot produce a both-endpoints retention failure over that path reduction.

Status: Established

Proof:

For each acyclic USO of $S$, record at every eligible attachment edge both directed singleton-out-map constraints and the corresponding undirected alternative. Exhaustive enumeration of all $2^{16}$ edge orientations finds $784$ acyclic USOs, independently reproduced from corner deletions: $69$ end at $0$, $69$ end at $8$, and $72$ have inward boundary at $T$. The pairwise intersections of the two endpoint records give $79$ distinct constraint sets, each contained in the record of a map retaining $T$. The general edge-attachment criterion converts endpoint peelings of the assembly into one of these common central constraint sets and converts the covering retaining map back into a peeling to $T$. Since every attachment is constant in coordinate $8$ and private blocks are disjoint, no attachment adds a doubled fiber to that reduction.
