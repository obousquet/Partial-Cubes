Repair Growth after Extending a Recovered Obstruction

Name: Repair Growth after Extending a Recovered Obstruction

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion
  • Coordinate Projection
  • Full Reduction

Result Type: Implication

Statement:

Let $H\subseteq Q_V$ be an irredundantly embedded ample forbidden pc-minor with a successful nonempty layer set $E$, and let $S=H\cup\{c\}$ be ample and nonpeelable. If $\mathcal R(Y)=\{a\notin Y:Y\cup\{a\}\text{ is dismantlable ample}\}$, then $\mathcal R(H)\subseteq\mathcal R(S)$, $|\mathcal R(S)\setminus\mathcal R(H)|\le1$, and $|\mathcal R(S)|\le3$. Every inherited repair is nonadjacent to $c$ and keeps its acquired support. A new repair, if present, has the same old-coordinate projection as $c$; their two missing layer words form an edge, so the new repair is adjacent to $c$. No signed-facet assembly over $S$ using compatible resolving tips is corner-descent terminal. Consequently, if a terminal facet assembly has a cornered seed, every obstruction-preserving corner deletion of that seed has no successful layer presentation.

Status: Established

Proof:

Recover $H$ from its successful layer set, with nonpeelable seed $K=H^E$. Since $S$ is nonpeelable, adding $c$ completes no full $E$-fibre, so $S^E=K$ and the acquired support of $c$ does not contain $E$. Every old repair has a different support; compatible-repair union makes $H\cup\{a,c\}$ ample, and deleting $c$ leaves a peelable family, so it is a repair of $S$ with unchanged support and is nonadjacent to $c$. Any new repair of $S$ must change the nonpeelable strong $E$-projection and therefore complete an $E$-fibre. Its old projection must equal that of $c$, or it would already repair $H$. The original fibre has exactly these two missing words; the recovered-row geometry makes them an edge. Hence there is at most one new repair, while the recovered-obstruction theorem bounds the inherited set by two. Finally, for the seed corner $c$, exactly the inherited repairs satisfy the seed-corner deletion criterion. At most one owner is ineligible, whereas opposite signed facets require distinct owners, so every compatible facet assembly retains an obstruction-preserving copy of $c$ and cannot be terminal.

Comments:

The bound of three repairs and the single-new-repair bound are both sharp. The theorem also explains why a cornered seed inside a terminal facet assembly cannot itself arise by an obstruction-preserving corner extension from an obstruction with successful layers.
