Successful Layer Sets Need Not Be Union-Closed for Peelable Ample Families

Name: Successful Layer Sets Need Not Be Union-Closed for Peelable Ample Families

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Coordinate Projection
  • Full Reduction

Result Type: Construction

Statement:

The obstruction hypothesis in union closure of successful layer sets is necessary. The ample, corner-peelable family $X=\{0,1,2,3,7,8\}\subseteq Q_4$ has balanced-coordinate set $\mathcal B(X)=\{0,1\}$. Each singleton $\{0\}$ and $\{1\}$ is a successful layer set, but their union is not successful because two pairs of recovery fibres collide.

Status: Established

Proof:

The family is a square with two pendant edges. Its shattered sets are the empty set, all four singletons, and $\{0,1\}$, so their number equals $|X|=6$ and the Sandwich equality proves ampleness. Its signed minimal nonfaces are $(12,12),(10,10),(9,9),(6,4),(5,4)$, which makes coordinates 0 and 1 balanced. Marking coordinate 0 produces the distinct singleton fibres $T_9=\{8\}$ and $T_5=\{6\}$; marking coordinate 1 produces $T_{10}=\{8\}$ and $T_6=\{5\}$. Thus both singleton markings recover valid presentations. Marking both gives $T_9=T_{10}=\{8\}$ and $T_5=T_6=\{4\}$, violating distinct-singleton recovery. A direct corner peeling shows that $X$ is dismantlable ample, so the obstruction theorem does not apply.
