One-Corner Extensions and the Two-Neighbor Falsifier

Name: One-Corner Extensions and the Two-Neighbor Falsifier

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes
  • Cornerless Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion
  • Coordinate Conditioning
  • Full Reduction

Result Type: Construction

Statement:

Let $G$ be an irredundantly embedded cornerless ample forbidden pc-minor. If adjacent words $c,h\notin G$ give ample one-concept extensions with the same acquired support, then $S=G\cup\{c\}$ is an ample forbidden pc-minor whose unique corner is $c$; it has no successful layer set and no common-seed cover with nonempty proper rows. More generally, suppose an irredundantly embedded ample forbidden pc-minor $S$ has unique corner $c$, and two distinct words $u,v\notin S$ adjacent to $c$ give dismantlable-ample extensions. On a new coordinate $e$, the two-section family $X=((S\cup\{u\})\times\{0\})\cup((S\cup\{v\})\times\{1\})$ is a corner-descent-terminal ample forbidden pc-minor with exactly two corners. Its only successful layer set is $\{e\}$ and its recovered seed is the cornered family $S$.

Status: Established

Proof:

Write $h=c^{\{f\}}$ and let $I$ be the common acquired support. Then $f\notin I$, and the two punctured $I$-cubes already lie in $G$. The completed $I$-cube makes $c$ a corner of $S$. Any other candidate corner in that cube has an $f$-neighbor supplied by the second punctured cube, while a corner outside it would already be a corner of $G$; hence $c$ is unique. Deleting it returns nonpeelable $G$, so the one-concept atlas makes $S$ forbidden. Every proper-row common-seed cover has at least two occurrence-row corners, excluding such a presentation and therefore any successful layer set. In the general construction, adjacency of the two repairs to different directions at $c$ forces their acquired supports to be distinct. The common-seed theorem makes $X$ forbidden, its two repair occurrences are its only corners, and deleting either exposes $S$ as a proper layer restriction, proving terminality. The new coordinate is thin and successful. The thin-layer dichotomy makes the largest presentation antipodally punctured; its two rows contribute at least twice its layer dimension in distinct corners. Since $X$ has only two corners, that dimension is one and $\{e\}$ is the unique successful set.

Comments:

The second part is conditional: the required two resolving neighbors are additional hypotheses. The fixed certificate finds adjacent ample additions to a 292-concept one-corner obstruction, but all three tested additions remain nonpeelable and therefore do not realize the falsifier.
