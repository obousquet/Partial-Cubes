The Eta-One Holonomy Anomaly Is a Tree of Squares

Name: The Eta-One Holonomy Anomaly Is a Tree of Squares

Classes:

  • Ample Classes

Result Type: Recognition

Statement:

Let a one-unit holonomy wing have $|K|\le9$, uncounted remainder $\eta=1$, and two disjoint two-concept cycle shores. Then $|L|\in\{1,2\}$. After choosing one collar vertex for each passive direction, exactly one vertex $v\in K$ remains uncounted, and $G(K)-v$ is obtained from the path $G(L)$ by attaching two pendant paths of length three. The square link of $v$ is a tree, so if $d=\deg_K(v)$, then $K$ contains exactly $d-1$ squares, all through $v$. For arm $i$, the pair $P_i=\{z_i,f_i\}$ of its attachment and terminal directions is not shattered by $K$; the two pairs are disjoint and are exactly the terminal-square supports acquired by the upper family.

Status: Established

Proof:

The one-unit identity gives $|K|=|L|+7$, and the shore-root lemma gives $1\le|L|\le2$. Connectivity and bipartiteness of each opposite active section allow its collar to be chosen so that the shore edge lifts to a pendant length-three arm. The two arms have disjoint nonbase vertices and no cross edge; together with $L$ they leave exactly the uncounted vertex $v$, so deleting $v$ gives the asserted tree and the one-vertex tree-completion theorem gives the square fan. Each passive arm direction occurs only on its attachment edge below, so a square through it cannot use the terminal direction; ampleness therefore excludes $P_i$ from $\operatorname{Sh}(K)$. Carrier connectivity forces the two terminal directions to differ, and the upper shore squares show that both pairs are acquired.
