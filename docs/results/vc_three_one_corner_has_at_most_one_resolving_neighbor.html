A VC-Three One-Corner Family Has at Most One Resolving Neighbor

Name: A VC-Three One-Corner Family Has at Most One Resolving Neighbor

Classes:

  • Ample Classes
  • Dismantlable Ample Classes

Operations:

  • Concept Deletion

Result Type: Implication

Statement:

Let $S\subseteq Q_F$ be ample and nonpeelable with unique corner $c$, and let $I$ be the incident-coordinate set of $c$. If $\operatorname{VCdim}(S)\le3$, at most one missing neighbor $u$ of $c$ has $S\cup\{u\}$ dismantlable ample. Hence the one-corner two-neighbor falsifier requires VC dimension at least four. Without a VC bound, if two resolving neighbors have three-coordinate acquired supports $J\cup\{i\}$ and $K\cup\{j\}$ with $J,K\subseteq I$, then $J\cup K\subsetneq I$. In the adjacent same-support companion construction with VC dimension at most three, the specified companion is the only possible resolving neighbor; two distinct companions exclude every resolving neighbor.

Status: Established

Proof:

The predecessor $G=S\setminus\{c\}$ is ample and nonpeelable, and adding $c$ acquires $I$. Low-rank invariance excludes resolving supports of size at most two. For two rank-three neighbors, the forced-deletion theorem gives two two-element sets $J,K$ and makes each peeling start with its associated first two old vertices. The only possible third deletion for the first repair uses some direction $r\in I\setminus J$ and forces a cube through an outside-$I$ neighbor. If $r\in K$, that cube either violates the second repair's private-edge condition or gives its first deletion an outside neighbor. Thus $r\notin J\cup K$, proving $J\cup K\subsetneq I$. Under VC dimension three, the corner cube forces $|I|=3$. Two rank-three supports contradict the strict-union condition; a rank-three and rank-four pair are excluded by the companion form of the forced-deletion theorem. If both supports have size four, deleting $c$ leaves a cornerless predecessor and three same-support tips, contradicting the VC-three third-companion theorem. The specialized companion assertions follow by the same rank bounds and that theorem.
