Median Graphs Are Strictly Contained in Convex-Geometry Ideals

Name: Median Graphs Are Strictly Contained in Convex-Geometry Ideals

Subject: Median Graphs

Object: Convex-Geometry Ideals

Relation: Strict Inclusion

Status: Established

Scope:

Finite one-inclusion graphs, up to signed-coordinate equivalence and suppression of constants.

Statement:

Every median graph is a convex-geometry ideal graph, and the inclusion is strict.

Witnesses:

  • Vertex-Deleted Three-Cube

Proof:

The inclusion is the convex-geometry representation theorem for median graphs. For strictness, $Q_3^- = Q_3\setminus\{111\}$ becomes the upper ideal $Q_3\setminus\{000\}$ after complementing all coordinates, so it belongs to $\mathsf{Icg}$; it is not median because the majority of $110,101,011$ is the missing vertex.
